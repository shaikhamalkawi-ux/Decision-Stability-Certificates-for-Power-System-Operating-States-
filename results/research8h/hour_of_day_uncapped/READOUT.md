# Finite fossil-electricity penalty for the two clock-preserving orders

Both fixed hour-of-day cases have accepted full binary/network dispatch witnesses after removing the fossil-energy cap. Their exact expanded-model energy upper bounds and exact LP-derived lower bounds give positive, finite optimum-to-optimum penalty intervals relative to the unchanged January identity model. There are two accepted witnesses in the two-case denominator. Neither MIP proved an optimum; both stopped at their 600-second limits. Independent post-run replay of points, dual bounds and interval arithmetic is required before publication.

All numbers below are outward-rounded display endpoints in MWh, or percentage points where marked. Exact rational endpoints in `energy_brackets.json` are authoritative.

These different upper endpoints must not be substituted into the optimum-to-optimum table.

The model change was exactly deletion of row 24264, the sole 23195 MWh fossil-energy cap, from each frozen capped target. Every other coefficient, row bound, finite column bound, original binary coordinate, native input and permutation remained unchanged. The objective is one per hourly P coordinate of each of the 23 native Coal/Oil/NG units, with zero weight on nuclear, other resources and state/angle coordinates. This is fossil **electrical output**, not fuel consumption, monetary cost or emissions. The same isolated 24-bus/38-line RTS Area 1 network, January week, weak initial/terminal convention and lack of named-unit means remain in force.

Only U was declared integral during the MIPs after the actual-matrix projection audit. Each accepted point recovers exact binary U and canonical Y/Z while preserving raw P/theta bytes. Both points pass the original full matrix, original 12096-coordinate U/Y/Z binary mask and independent native no-cap physical checker under the declared uniform expansion. Both fail strict nominal exact membership by small binary64 residuals, so **the table concerns the uniformly expanded original-binary models**, not exact feasibility for the unexpanded nominal model or measured physical uncertainty.

The 60-second LP calls use the same uncapped matrices and energy objectives with continuous variables. Both returned numerical Optimal status, but the proof uses exact Fraction arithmetic on their archived row multipliers and the finite-box stationarity correction. Invalid endpoint-sign entries are projected before recomputation. For q=c−A^T d, the nominal lower bound includes the exact minimum of q over the complete variable box; the expanded lower subtracts tau times `||d||1+||q||1`, with tau exactly `Fraction.from_float(1e-5)`. No exact stationarity or solver optimality is assumed. No expanded bound is clipped at zero. Raw/projected duals and exact residuals are archived separately. A continuous LP primal is not used as a binary upper bound.

The two MIP calls took 600.2120261 and 600.1937997 seconds. Their final numerical relative gaps were approximately 1.7501% and 0.8220%; these numerical gaps are diagnostics, not the rigorous penalty intervals above. The two LP calls took 1.7847879 and 1.2583708 seconds. The solve/check phase took 1215.869198 seconds, with no 2100-second soft allocation or 04:00 UTC overrun. The individual MIP soft limits overran by approximately 0.212 and 0.194 seconds, as retained in the logs. There were exactly two MIP calls and two LP calls, no warm starts, no retries, no new seeds and no identity solve. All 92 frozen bindings passed before and after execution and during arithmetic-only summary generation.

The earlier capped models remain exact robust negatives. These new witnesses show finite uncapped feasibility for the same orders, and the bounds locate a strictly positive fossil-electricity penalty for the two specified expanded models. The experiment preserves hour of day and joint hourly packages but can change within-day adjacency and weather continuity. It is an exploratory sensitivity result within the same week/network, not additional replication, population evidence, a general theorem about weather or an operating recommendation.
