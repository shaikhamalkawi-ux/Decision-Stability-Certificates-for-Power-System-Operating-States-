\section{Scope and provenance}
This supplement provides computational details for the chronology, alternating-current (AC) restoration, Great Britain, Elexon operational-data, and independent numerical-verification analyses reported in the main article. The RTS calculations use the same admitted operating-law states and source-processing definitions throughout the study, with RTS-GMLC pinned to source commit \texttt{3ece0d3725c844056132393ee252b3083dd4eab4} \cite{Barrows2020,RTSRepo}. The chronology and AC calculations add constraints and validation layers while preserving the static mean, shape, hour-conditioned, and fixed-commitment state definitions. We use direct-current (DC) network constraints for chronological dispatch, mixed-integer linear programs (MILPs) for commitment, linear programs (LPs) for continuous relaxations, and nonlinear programs (NLPs) for AC projection.

\section{Native chronology formulation}
Let $g$ index the 41 admitted generation coordinates and $t=0,\ldots,T-1$ the hourly periods, with $T=168$. Active generation $p_{g,t}$ is measured in MW. For thermal units, $P_g^{\min}$ is minimum online output, $P_{g,t}^{\max}$ is hourly available maximum output, $u_{g,t}\in\{0,1\}$ is on/off status, and $y_{g,t},z_{g,t}\in\{0,1\}$ are startup/shutdown indicators. The independent verification formulation imposes the power/status link at every hour and the transition equations only for $t=1,\ldots,T-1$:
\begin{align}
P_g^{\min}u_{g,t}&\le p_{g,t}\le P_{g,t}^{\max}u_{g,t},\\
u_{g,t}-u_{g,t-1}&=y_{g,t}-z_{g,t},\\
y_{g,t}+z_{g,t}&\le1,
\end{align}
For source minimum-up time $U_g$ and minimum-down time $D_g$ in hours, observed starts and stops satisfy, for $t=1,\ldots,T-1$,
\begin{align}
\sum_{\tau=\max(1,t-U_g+1)}^{t} y_{g,\tau}&\le u_{g,t},\\
\sum_{\tau=\max(1,t-D_g+1)}^{t} z_{g,\tau}&\le 1-u_{g,t}.
\end{align}
The integers $U_g$ and $D_g$ are the source \texttt{Min Up Time Hr} and \texttt{Min Down Time Hr} fields (rounded upward only if a noninteger were encountered). Hourly active-power balance is enforced; hydro availability follows the retained source-processing treatment used for the static baseline. The boundary is intentionally weak: $u_{g,0}$ is free, $y_{g,0}=z_{g,0}=0$, and no transition to a pre-horizon status is imposed. Residence-time constraints apply only within the observed week, with no terminal extension. This makes the gate a relaxation, so exact-mean infeasibility is inherited by any stricter network-constrained chronology.

The native online-to-online ramp-only gate uses \texttt{Ramp Rate MW/Min}$\times60$ for the hourly interval. Unit-commitment (UC) benchmarks can impose different ramp, startup and boundary semantics \cite{PGLibUC,Knueven2020}; the reported chronology conclusions here use the pinned native RTS parameters throughout. \section{Chronological mean repair}
For a target vector of mean generator outputs $\mu_T$, let $\mu_{T,g}$ be its coordinate for generator $g$ and let $d_g^+,d_g^-\ge0$ be positive and negative mean deviations in MW. The repair problem minimizes their sum, equal at optimality to the $L_1$ deviation, where $\|v\|_1=\sum_g|v_g|$:
\begin{equation}
\sum_g(d_g^+ + d_g^-),\qquad
\frac1T\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} p_{g,t}-d_g^++d_g^-=\mu_{T,g},
\end{equation}
subject to the relaxed chronology. July solves to an exact objective of 8.0205084643 MW with reported mixed-integer-program (MIP) gap zero. March does not have a certified integer incumbent within the declared solver budget; its LP relaxation yields 40.0187592756 MW, so that number is a lower bound only.

For July, the stricter Area-1 DC network chronology is also bounded directly. Let $A_{\mathrm{chron}}^{\mathrm{net+ramp}}(\mu_{\mathrm{Jul}})$ denote the minimum $L_1$ mean deviation from the July target under nodal DC balance, all 38 internal continuous line ratings, source-native minimum up/down logic, and native online-to-online ramping. The certified interval, with endpoints rounded outward, is
\begin{equation}
8.0205084642\le A_{\mathrm{chron}}^{\mathrm{net+ramp}}(\mu_{\mathrm{Jul}})\le11.9262964643\ \mathrm{MW}.
\end{equation}
The lower endpoint is inherited from the exact no-network relaxation. The upper endpoint is not an optimality claim: it is an explicit feasible schedule obtained after a bounded network MIP search and status-neighborhood refinement. A separate verifier recomputes the schedule from the source data and records maximum system-balance residual $6.6\times10^{-12}$ MW, maximum branch-loading fraction $1.0000000000000002$, zero active-power bound violation, zero native online-ramp excess, and zero minimum-up/down violations. The exact network optimum remains unresolved inside the interval.

\section{Direct AC feasibility projection}
The AC projection uses the Area-1 internal 24-bus/38-branch topology. At bus $i$, let $V_i$ be voltage magnitude in per-unit (p.u.), $\theta_i$ its phase angle in radians, and $v_i=V_i e^{j\theta_i}$ its complex voltage phasor, with $j^2=-1$. Let $Y_{ik}$ be an entry of the per-unit bus-admittance matrix, $\mathcal G_i$ the set of generators at bus $i$, $P_g,Q_g$ generator active/reactive power, and $P_i^d,Q_i^d$ active/reactive demand. With active power in MW, reactive power in MVAr, and base power $S_{\mathrm{base}}=100$ MVA, the complex nodal balance is
\begin{equation}
\sum_{g\in\mathcal G_i}(P_g+jQ_g)-(P_i^d+jQ_i^d)
=S_{\mathrm{base}}v_i\overline{\sum_kY_{ik}v_k}.
\end{equation}
The overbar denotes complex conjugation and $k$ indexes buses. For the DC target generator output $P_g^{\mathrm{DC}}$, the restoration objective is $\sum_g|P_g-P_g^{\mathrm{DC}}|$ in MW. The nonlinear program imposes source hourly $P$ availability, source generator $Q$ limits, $0.95\le V_i\le1.05$ p.u., and apparent-power limits at both branch terminals: $|S_{ik}|\le S^{\rm cont}_{ik}$ and $|S_{ki}|\le S^{\rm cont}_{ik}$. Here $S_{ik}$ is complex power injected into the branch at terminal $i$, and $S^{\rm cont}_{ik}$ is the continuous MVA rating. The implementation adds the numerical voltage regularizer $10^{-8}\sum_i(V_i-1)^2$, whose value is at most $6\times10^{-10}$ over the declared voltage interval and 24 buses. Reported restoration costs exclude that term. Rooftop photovoltaic generation is fixed negative active load, and bus reactive demand is scaled by the same regional load factor used for active demand. CasADi and IPOPT provide the primary witness search \cite{Andersson2019,Wachter2006}; successful points are checked against all constraint residuals. For hour $t$, $\widehat R_t^{\mathrm{AC}}$ denotes the returned $L_1$ witness cost, which upper-bounds the ideal minimum $R_t^{\mathrm{AC}}$ for a feasible returned point.

This direct projection differs from a heuristic recovery pipeline using distributed slack and switching between active-power/voltage-controlled (PV) buses and active/reactive-power-specified (PQ) buses. The latter is well motivated in the literature \cite{Boateng2027}, but here $P$, $Q$, voltage, and branch limits are optimized jointly. The benefit is a direct feasible witness; the nonconvex program provides a locally computed restoration value rather than a globally certified minimum. \section{Eight-week chronology and independent explanation}
The fixed seasonal protocol includes all eight archived weeks: January, February, March, April, May, June, July and October. The same 41 target coordinate means and native hourly inputs are used. All eight no-network minimum-up/down models terminate Infeasible with HiGHS 1.12.0, a 60-s limit, one thread and seed zero. Every archived static schedule itself passes native online-to-online ramps. For every thermal unit, let $r_g$ be its native ramp rate in MW/min and $P_g^{\max}$ its constant available maximum. Then $60r_g\ge P_g^{\max}-P_g^{\min}$, so this on/on-only family is redundant at hourly resolution; the smallest slack is 30 MW. This does not address startup/shutdown limits or a finer time grid. A May ramp search timed out without an incumbent, but its original schedule is a directly verified witness. Both records are retained.

An exploratory independent check explains the rejection without an optimization solver. For thermal unit $g$ with $P_g^{\min}>0$, let $\Delta t=1$ h and $E_g=\Delta t\sum_t p^{\mathrm{target}}_{g,t}$ MWh. Propagate necessary lower and upper hourly generation bounds using aggregate balance, each coordinate's fixed energy, generator availability, fixed hydro and the thermal off-or-online-output gap. A positive necessary lower bound forces a unit on; an upper bound below its minimum online output forces it off. For each unit, a finite-state dynamic program stores the current status, remaining mandatory dwell and the reachable total online-hour counts. Both initial statuses start with no prior obligation, transitions add the native minimum dwell, and remaining terminal obligations are not extended beyond hour 167. Let $\mathcal N_g$ be the resulting reachable count set. Feasibility requires
\[
\mathcal N_g\cap\left[\left\lceil\frac{E_g}{P_g^{\max}\Delta t}\right\rceil,
\left\lfloor\frac{E_g}{P_g^{\min}\Delta t}\right\rfloor\right]\ne\varnothing.
\]
The implementation widens power bounds by $10^{-5}$ MW and coordinate energy by $168\times10^{-5}$ MWh; it never infers feasibility from this necessary condition. Exhaustive enumeration of all binary schedules verifies the recurrence on 1125 short-horizon forced-status cases.

Seven weeks fail this independent test. For example, January's \texttt{123\_STEAM\_3} target energy is 3228.521078 MWh, allowing at most 23 online hours at its 140-MW minimum output; the forced statuses and native 24-h up/48-h down times require at least 100 online hours. In July, \texttt{115\_STEAM\_3} is forced on at hour 2, off at hour 3 and on at hour 7, violating its eight-hour minimum downtime. June is not rejected by this single-unit test; its full mixed-integer result remains the evidence for rejection. The January source-week rejection also means that no chronologically feasible initial operating law is assumed.

\section{Fixed eight-day Elexon sensitivity}
The protocol selects every Wednesday of January and July 2023 before downloading additional dates: January 4, 11, 18, 25 and July 5, 12, 19, 26. Its four ordinal pairs are primary and its 16 cross-pairs reuse days; they are descriptive comparisons, not independent samples for significance testing. Each vector retains signed B1610 average-MW values. The acquisition-date production classification includes pumped-storage-coded units and station-demand names; no negative value is clipped. A duplicated registry identifier with consistent classification is represented once as a coordinate, with its two Energy Identification Code (EIC) aliases retained in diagnostics.

Pairwise complete intersections have 259--260 coordinates; the common intersection of all days has 259. Primary shape shares are 6.4889\%, 5.8873\%, 6.5885\% and 13.5601\% in ordinal order; same-period premiums range from 1.15345\% to 2.46693\%. Over all 16 cross-pairs, shape shares range from 3.46077\% to 13.56012\% and same-period premiums from 0.74634\% to 9.35493\%. The fixed-coordinate sensitivity gives primary shape shares of 5.89130--13.56628\%. All 32 independent transportation-program checks agree with assignment results within $1.1\times10^{-11}$ MW. Original-date quantities, run types and keys are unchanged on reacquisition despite different raw byte hashes. Numerical reproducibility is asserted within declared tolerances; byte identity requires the locked serialization environment. Two months and one weekday cannot establish annual, causal or weather-adjusted robustness.
